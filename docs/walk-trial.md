# The new walk: the first floor try

What to do the first time Kilo walks the new walk (remote key C4, as
before), what to watch, how to stop him, and the one measurement worth
taking. Write what you saw in [walk-trial-results.md](walk-trial-results.md);
"don't know" is a fine answer. The design and its numbers are
[walking-and-balance.md](walking-and-balance.md) section 16.

**What changed.** The old walk lifted a back leg and, 60 ms later, the
front leg on the other side, so his feet landed in quick pairs ("da-dum
... da-dum") and for part of every step he stood on only two feet: short,
choppy steps, the "stutter". The new walk is the way a cat walks slowly:
one leg at a time, always in the same order (left back, left front, right
back, right front), a foot coming down every quarter of a second, evenly.
He always has at least three feet on the floor. Each step is longer (43 mm
instead of 25 at the default speed) and he takes half as many. His feet
are also placed a little further forward than before (7 mm), so that his
weight sits in the middle of them.

It is a little slower than the old walk: 58 mm a second at the default
(the old one: 80). The trot covers the faster speeds.

It first walked on the floor on 2026-11-22 (boot 16): the recording's
numbers are in walk-trial-results.md. What it looked like has not been
written down yet, and the side video below has not been taken.

## Before you start

- The mind on Kilo must be a build with the new walk (from 2026-11-22 on).
  Claude tells you when it is there.
- A charged battery.
- **Clear, flat floor, at least 2 m long and 1 m wide.** He walks about
  1 m every 17 seconds at the default speed. A hard floor (wood, tile) is
  better than a rug.
- A tape measure laid flat on the floor along his path, numbers facing up,
  for the video (below).
- A phone that records slow motion, if it has it.

## The try

1. Put Kilo at one end of the clear floor, facing along the tape.
2. **B6**: he stands.
3. Start the phone's video (see "The measurement").
4. **C4**: he starts walking forward at the default speed (58 mm a
   second). His steps grow to their full length over the first second.
5. If that looks steady, **B5** twice: the slowest speed (23 mm a second).
   This is the gentlest he walks.
6. **B7** three times: back to the default and one step faster (67 mm a
   second). Only if that looks steady too, **B7** once more: the top
   (73 mm a second).
7. **B6** to stop him, well before the end of the floor.
8. If all of that went well and you like: **C4** again, then **B5** four
   times (he slows down, then walks backward, slowly), a few steps, **B6**.
   Then **A6** (he turns left on the spot in the walk) for a few seconds,
   **B6**.

Take as long as you like between the steps; nothing is waiting on a timer.

## How to stop him

- **B6** stops him: his foot in the air comes down where it is and he is
  still in about half a second, then he squares his legs and stands.
- **C7** (freeze) stops him the same way and then ignores everything but B6
  and A7.
- **Picking him up is fine**, at any moment: the walk ends on its own.
- The distance guard stops him by itself if something is close ahead, and
  if something comes very close it walks him backward a little, as before.
  Do not count on it for the end of the floor: stop him with B6.

## What to watch for

- Does it look smoother than the old walk, or not? Steadier body, calmer
  steps? This is the question that matters most; your impression is the
  answer.
- Does he rock from side to side? More or less than the old walk?
- Does he stumble or tip toward a foot that is in the air? Which foot, if
  you can tell?
- Does a foot scrape or drag on the floor as it swings forward?
- Does he walk straight, or drift to one side?
- Does it sound different (the motors straining, buzzing)?

## The measurement: a video from the side

- The phone at floor level (lying on the floor or on a book), about 1 m to
  the side of his path, pointing across it. Slow motion if the phone has
  it, otherwise a normal video.
- The tape measure on the floor along his path, in view.
- Film him walking past at the default speed (step 4) for a few steps.

What it shows: whether the feet really come down one at a time and evenly,
how far each one travels, and whether the body stays level or dips toward
the foot in the air. The walk's recording (the mind keeps every walk;
Claude can fetch it afterwards) gives the body's rocking, which can be
compared number for number with the old walk's.

Write in the results file: the date, which speeds he walked, what you saw
for each point above, and where the video is.
