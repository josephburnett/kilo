# The long trot: the first floor try

What to do the first time Kilo walks the long trot (remote key A1), what to
watch, how to stop him, and the one measurement worth taking. Write what
you saw in [long-trot-trial-results.md](long-trot-trial-results.md);
"don't know" is a fine answer. The design and its numbers are
[walking-and-balance.md](walking-and-balance.md) section 15.

**What the long trot is.** Today's trot, but each foot stays in the air a
little longer (the "swing": 180 to 220 ms instead of 160), so it can be
carried further without the leg's motors turning faster than they safely
can. The step ("stride": how far a foot travels in one step) gets longer:
34 to 50 mm instead of the 30 to 38 the trot takes at these speeds. He takes fewer,
longer steps. It is no faster than today's fast trot. It starts as an
ordinary trot and lengthens the steps over the first second.

The long trot has never walked on the floor: everything about it so far is
a model and a simulation.

## Before you start

- The mind on Kilo must be a build with the long trot (from 2026-11-21 on).
  Claude tells you when it is there; until then A1 does nothing.
- A charged battery.
- **Clear, flat floor in a straight line, at least 4 m long and 1 m wide.**
  He walks about 1 m every 5 to 6 seconds (160 to 200 mm a second), and
  the try below uses about 3 m. A hard floor (wood, tile) is better than a
  rug: a rug can catch the longer step.
- A tape measure laid flat on the floor along his path, numbers facing up,
  for the video (below).
- A phone that records slow motion, if it has it.

## The try

1. Put Kilo at one end of the clear floor, facing along the tape.
2. **B6**: he stands.
3. Start the phone's slow-motion video (see "The measurement").
4. **A1**: he starts trotting forward at stage 1 (160 mm a second). Over
   the first second his steps get longer.
5. If stage 1 looks steady, **B7**: stage 2 (180 mm a second, a little
   longer step). This is the stage the measurement wants. Let him walk at
   it for as much of the floor as you have; about 2 m is plenty.
6. Only if stage 2 looks steady too, and there is floor left, **B7** again:
   stage 3 (200 mm a second, the longest step).
7. **B6** to stop him, well before the end of the floor.

To go back to the ordinary trot without stopping: **B5** goes down a stage,
and at stage 1 into the trot. **A6 / C6** bend his path a little (one step
only in the long trot).

## How to stop him

- **B6** stops him: his feet come down where they are and he is still in
  about half a second, then he squares his legs and stands.
- **C7** (freeze) stops him the same way and then ignores everything but B6
  and A7.
- **Picking him up is fine**, at any moment: the walk ends on its own.
- The distance guard stops him by itself if something is close ahead
  (about 20 cm), as in the trot. Do not count on it for the end of the
  floor: stop him with B6.

## What to watch for

- Does he stumble, trip or fall? At which stage? Did the trouble start as
  the steps got longer?
- Does a foot scrape or drag on the floor as it swings forward, or catch
  on something?
- Does he rock from side to side more than in the ordinary trot, and does
  the rocking grow or stay the same?
- Does he walk straight, or drift to one side?
- Does it sound different (the motors straining, buzzing)?

## The measurement: a slow-motion side video

The one measurement that settles the most: a slow-motion video from the
side, with the tape measure in view.

- The phone at floor level (lying on the floor or on a book), about 1 m to
  the side of his path, pointing across it, so you see him side-on as he
  walks past. 240 frames a second if the phone has it, otherwise its
  slow-motion setting.
- The tape measure on the floor along his path, between him and the phone
  or just behind him, so the steps can be read against it.
- Film him walking past at stage 2 (180 mm a second) for a few steps.
  Stage 1 and stage 3 too if you have them, as separate clips or one clip.

What it shows: how far each foot really travels in a step (the plan asks
41 mm at stage 2), how high it lifts, whether it lands where it should, and
how early the foot on the side he leans toward touches down. The walk's
recording (the mind keeps every walk; Claude can fetch it afterwards) gives
the sway; the video gives the feet.

Write in the results file: the date, which stages he walked, what you saw
for each point above, and where the video is.
